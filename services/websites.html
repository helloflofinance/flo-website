The problem

Most small-business sites are a dead end.

They look fine. They load slowly on a phone, which is where nearly everyone sees them. They say what the business does and then stop — no booking, no form, nothing to do next but find an email address and hope.

Then there's the other failure: a template so generic that it makes a genuinely good operator look interchangeable with every other result on the page.

A site earns its keep when somebody lands on it and books. Everything else is decoration.

Questions

Before you book.

What does a website cost?

Launch packages start at $350 and the full ladder is published on the pricing page — no quote-to-find-out. Bigger builds with automation or AI wired in are scoped on the call and sent as one fixed number.

How long until it's live?

A launch package is usually days, not months. Larger builds depend on how much content and integration is involved; you get the timeline in writing before work starts.

Do I need to write all the copy?

No. Bring what you have and flo writes the rest from the discovery call. You approve every word before it ships.

Who hosts it, and can I edit it?

You own the site and the hosting account. Hand-over covers how to make everyday edits yourself, and ongoing support is available monthly if you'd rather not.

Start with the intake.

Thirty minutes on where your time and money actually go. You leave with a plan whether or not we work together — that part is free, and it is not a pitch.

Booking asks a few intake questions first — what is eating your week, what you already run on — so the call starts with context instead of a blank page.

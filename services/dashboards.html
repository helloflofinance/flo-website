The problem

The numbers exist. Nobody can see them in one place.

Revenue is in one system, jobs in another, costs in a spreadsheet somebody rebuilds every month. Getting a straight answer to how did we actually do takes half a day, so it gets asked quarterly instead of weekly.

By the time the picture is assembled it's describing a month you can no longer do anything about.

A dashboard is worth building when it changes a decision — which means it has to be short, current, and about the handful of numbers an owner actually acts on.

Questions

Before you book.

Why Power BI and not something else?

Cost and reach. At small-business scale Power BI is the cheapest capable option, and it connects to nearly every system a small operator already runs. If your stack genuinely points somewhere else, flo will tell you on the call rather than sell you the thing it prefers.

Do I need clean data before we start?

No. Cleaning and joining the messy sources is most of the work, and it's part of the build.

How often does it refresh?

Whatever the decision needs — daily for most operators, more often where the source supports it. Set during scoping, not guessed at.

Who keeps it running afterwards?

You own it and the hand-over covers how it works. Ongoing support is available monthly if you'd rather flo kept an eye on it.
